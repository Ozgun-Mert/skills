# Professional pages

Trust pages make a site look like a real business to people and to search engines: who is behind it,
how to reach them, and how data is handled. They also catch "brand + contact/address/privacy"
searches.

## 1. Which pages

| Site type | Pages to propose |
|---|---|
| Product / SaaS / company | About, Contact, Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, Cookie Policy (only if cookies or tracking exist), Security, KVKK Aydınlatma Metni (Turkey) or a GDPR section (EU users), optional Status/Changelog |
| Local service business (lawyer, mediator, clinic, restaurant) | About (credentials, experience), Services, Contact (address, map, hours, phone), Privacy/KVKK, optional FAQ |
| Personal site / portfolio | About, Contact, links to socials (also as `sameAs` in Person JSON-LD), Portfolio or Services, Privacy if there is a contact form or analytics |

Check what already exists before proposing anything. Add these to the page plan table like any other
page: they go through the same approval.

**Contact pages** must use the same name, address and phone (NAP) everywhere: page copy, footer and
LocalBusiness/Organization JSON-LD. Mismatches confuse local search. Use placeholders if you don't
know them.

## 2. Legal page question (ask once per legal page)

Use AskUserQuestion when available:

- question: "Should I write the <Privacy Policy> page (`/<path>`)?"
- options:
  - **Yes**: write it from what I know about the product (after asking the legal facts below)
  - **Yes with banner**: same, plus a visible banner saying it's AI-generated and needs a
    lawyer's review
  - **No**: don't create this page

Legal pages here are Terms, Privacy, Cookies, KVKK/GDPR notices, Security (when it makes promises),
Refund/Cancellation and Distance Sales (Mesafeli Satış) pages. About and Contact are not legal pages.

## 3. Legal facts to collect before writing (ask only what's unknown)

1. Legal entity name, type and registered address (or the person's name for a sole trader)
2. Country of the company, and where users/customers are (Turkey → KVKK; EU/EEA → GDPR; California → CCPA …)
3. What personal data is collected (forms, accounts, payments, analytics, cookies) and why
4. Where data is stored and processed (hosting provider and region)
5. Sub-processors: analytics, payment, email, hosting, error tracking, chat
6. Cookies: which ones, and whether there is a consent banner
7. Retention periods
8. Minimum age / children's data
9. Contact for data requests (email, address; KEP address in Turkey if any)
10. For Terms: paid plans, refunds, cancellation, liability limits, governing law and jurisdiction

Never fill these with guesses. Unknown facts become placeholders (`{{PLACEHOLDER: company_legal_name}}`)
and the page stays "needs review" in the state file.

## 4. AI banner (for "Yes with banner")

Place it at the very top of the main content, visible (not hidden, not dismissible), in the page's
language. Use a semantic element such as `<aside role="note">` styled with the site's existing alert
or notice style.

- TR: **"Bu metin yapay zekâ tarafından oluşturulmuştur ve yayına alınmadan önce bir avukat tarafından incelenmelidir."**
- EN: **"This text was generated by AI and must be reviewed by a lawyer before going to production."**
- Other languages: translate faithfully.

Record `banner: true` for that page in the state file, and list it under the report's open items.

## 5. SEO handling of legal pages

Legal pages should be indexable (they build trust) but they're low priority: include them in the
sitemap and link them from the footer, never put them in the main nav, and use concise unique titles
("Gizlilik Politikası | Brand").
